## Verdicts

| Check | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **1. No tuning (Rule 10)** | **Confirmed.** `constants.ts` is untouched. The calculation is U·A·ΔT, ρc·n·V·ΔT and a stated W/m²; it has no coefficient that could be fitted. The gate (±3 % room, ±2 % building) is the brief's. 0.5 ach and 4 m/s are round and stated. The three air-density runs before the gated one are listed, with their results, and the one kept is the spec's own at-site rate (Brief 104). |
| **2. The "combined" referee: legitimate, or goalpost-moving?** | **Legitimate as an arithmetic check, but it is the weaker of the two tests, and the reason given for the iso_conv miss is wrong in its conclusion (F1).** What "combined" does is make EnergyPlus use the method's own surface films and switch long-wave off. Fabric heat in a steady state is then U·A·ΔT by construction, so the run can only fail on areas, constructions, bridging, the ground, partitions, or air. That is still worth having: EnergyPlus builds those from the spec independently, and they agree within 1.8 %. But it cannot test the method's *convention*, only its arithmetic, and the Part 1 hand calculations already test the arithmetic. The gate was not loosened and the switch is stated openly, so I would not call it goalpost-moving. It should be named for what it is ("arithmetic referee") and an operative-temperature run added as the physical one (F1). |
| **3. The iso_conv miss** | **Mostly a control mismatch, not "the method is on the safe side".** In iso_conv, EnergyPlus holds the *air* at 20 °C. Probe P-a reproduces EnergyPlus's west-room fabric heat (1,115 W against EnergyPlus's 1,143 W, 2.4 %) and shows that with the air at 20 °C the inside faces average about 15.7 °C, so the operative temperature is about **17.9 °C**. Held at **20 °C operative** instead, the air must be about 22.4 °C and the room needs about **1,557 W** (fabric 1,236 + air 321), against the engine's 1,588 W: **+2.0 %**, inside the room gate. So the "+9.9 %" compares the design figure with a room that is 2 K colder to sit in. |
| **4. Sources and labelling (published UK sources only)** | **Nothing from BS EN 12831-1 is reproduced.** I found no formula, factor or table from the standard in the code, the tests or the reports: only its name, where MIS 3005-D and the MCS calculator cite it. The 12 m³/h·m² default the reconnaissance mentions is quoted from the MCS calculator's own page. **But four labels are wrong or missing (F2–F5):** the clause number (MIS 3005-D Issue 3.0 has these rules in cl. 5.5.1, not 3.4.1); the 0.5 ach minimum is the MCS *calculator's* (CIBSE DHDG 2026 Table 2-18), not MIS 3005-D's, which has no air-change minimum at all; suspended floors are treated as solid without the screen saying so, although MIS 3005-D gives them a different rule; and the brief's P5 CLAUDE.md line still says "BS EN 12831-1 transmission, ventilation and reheat". |
| **5. Air: 0.5 ach, ventilation at its largest hour, vents at a design wind** | **Sound and consistent with the year's run, with two caveats (F6, F7).** Infiltration is the model's own n (q50/20, or a stated ach) and is constant, as in the year's run. Mechanical ventilation at its largest hourly conductance is its full flow after recovery: `ventilation.ts` refuses summer bypass, so the largest hour cannot be a bypass hour. The 0.5 ach top-up counts infiltration, vents and the full mechanical flow (before recovery), which matches "air changes"; it applies only to a room with an outside surface, as the MCS calculator says. Caveats: the referee gives EnergyPlus the engine's own design flows, so the air term is checked for ρc only, not for the flows (F6); and 4 m/s has no stated basis and the wind is not printed on the panel (F7). |
| **6. Rule 9** | **Holds, and the reconciliation is to the integrand.** Each room's total is the sum of its lines (`designHeatLoss.ts:180`); the seam groups lines by term without dropping any; the panel lists every non-zero term and the total. The figure compared with EnergyPlus is the total less the two lines EnergyPlus does not model (top-up, warm-up), and both of those are hand-checked in Part 1 (a). |
| **7. Rule 11** | **Holds.** Areas, U-values, bridging and infiltration come from `ZoneResults.conductances`; the ground temperature is moved into one exported `groundTemperature()` and the year's run now calls it too (a pure refactor). `summary.design_heat_loss` is the single exposure; `DesignHeatLoss.tsx` and `EmitterSizing.tsx` both read it and recompute nothing. `w_per_m2` and `building_w` are worked out once in the engine and copied, not recomputed, in the seam. The emitters reuse 108's `zoneEmitters`/`zoneSizing` on a copy of the document with the setpoint at the design temperature (mutation M8 is caught). |
| **8. Today's engine and the anchor** | **Untouched; the number is owed (F10).** No file under `utils/`, `nza_engine/`, `api/` or `pages/` changes. `model.ts`'s change is Simulate v2's engine, a pure extract of the ground mean. So nothing can move the anchor, but 133.2 itself was not re-run here (no EPW); it is owed at P5 on IT15, as in 109. |

## What is good

- The refusals are complete and by name, and nothing is filled: the 2.5 → 2.6 upgrade adds nulls only, and without a design block the panel says what to state.
- The ground temperature was moved into one function rather than computed twice.
- Both referee runs are kept and reported side by side, with the switch said plainly. That is the right way to handle a result you did not expect; the only fault is the conclusion drawn from it (F1).
- The panel's emitter numbers check by hand with the log-mean ΔT that `emitters.ts` uses: 70/60/21 °C gives 43.8 K and 50/40/21 °C gives 23.65 K against 49.83 K rated, so (43.8/49.83)^1.3 × 30 kW = 25.4 kW and (23.65/49.83)^1.3 × 30 kW = 11.4 kW, as the panel shows (25,374 W and 11,384 W). The sense check in `03_panel.md` prints 44.0 and 23.9 K; the figures are right, the printed ΔTs are slightly off (a wording fix).
