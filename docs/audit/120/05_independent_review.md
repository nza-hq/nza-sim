# Brief 120 — independent review

16 Nov 2026. Branch `chris/120-plant-capacity`, commits bbc3a36..397d618 (P1, P2, P2b, the water heater, P3, P4), stacked on Brief 112. This is a docs-only review: it changed no code. The builder answers in `05_review_response.md`.

**What was run here:**
- The v2 suites (`src/thermal`, `src/sim2`): 421 pass, 2 skip.
- The fast flow search against the slow bisection it replaced, on 4,900 cases.
- An under- and over-delivery probe over every short step of five plant cases.
- The fixed split with a capped heat pump beside an uncapped boiler.
- Six mutations of the new code.

**Not run here:**
- EnergyPlus.
- The community centre. Its plan is on `chris/example-model` and its weather is not in the container.
- Bridgewater's anchor.

The numbers in `02_referee.md` and `04_example.md` are taken as recorded.

## Verdict

**The plant limit is built correctly, and P2b is physics, not fitting.** Three checks support that:
- Sharing through one lower flow temperature is the mechanism EnergyPlus's loop uses, with no new constant.
- The fast solver is exact to 1e-9.
- A short plant gives exactly what it has in every short step.

**Three things should be settled before close:**
- **The fixed split (F1).** It holds an uncapped boiler back when a heat pump beside it is capped. With a 0.6 kW heat pump and an *unlimited* gas boiler, each room goes 1,823 hours unmet. That is not what a bivalent system does, and Linzie should choose the rule.
- **Decision 1 (F2).** The decision card asked Linzie about sharing in proportion to ask. That is no longer what runs on wet systems.
- **The "system short / own" split (F3).** It relabels the rooms' own hours as "system short". So `04_example.md`'s claim that the café and hall are "only ever short because of the boiler" is contradicted by its own uncapped row.

| F1 | **Medium–High** | **A capped heat pump holds back an uncapped boiler beside it.** Under the P3 fixed split, a short system with share s that can give A holds the room's whole heating to A ÷ s. The other systems' shares fall with it, even when they have capacity to spare. The builder's note calls topping up "lead/lag, out of scope". But a heat pump with a boiler beside it is the commonest bivalent arrangement, and there the boiler covers what the heat pump cannot. As built, adding an unlimited boiler beside a small heat pump does not reduce the rooms' unmet hours, and the boiler burns *less* gas because the heat pump is small.
